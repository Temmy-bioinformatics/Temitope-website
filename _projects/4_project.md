Different parts of the genome can retain conflicting signals of evolutionary history, particularly when closely related species have experienced hybridization and gene flow. In this ongoing project, I integrate **mitochondrial and nuclear genomic data** to investigate hybridization, introgression, cytonuclear discordance, and species boundaries across Eurasian minnows (*Phoxinus*).

### What I am working on

I combine population genomic, phylogenomic, and species-delimitation approaches to disentangle evolutionary relationships among closely related *Phoxinus* lineages. Key analyses include:

- **Genome-wide population structure and ancestry analysis** to characterise genetic relationships and identify patterns of admixture
- **Mitochondrial phylogenetics** to reconstruct maternal evolutionary relationships
- **Nuclear phylogenomics** to infer genome-wide relationships among species and populations
- **Introgression analysis** to identify patterns of historical gene flow between divergent lineages
- **Phylogenetic topology weighting** using TWISST to quantify variation in evolutionary relationships across the genome
- **QuIBL analysis** to distinguish discordance consistent with incomplete lineage sorting from patterns associated with introgression
- **Species delimitation** to evaluate alternative hypotheses about evolutionary boundaries within the species complex
- Integration of mitochondrial and nuclear ancestry to investigate **cytonuclear discordance and hybridization**

### When genomes tell different stories

Mitochondrial and nuclear genomes can retain different signals of evolutionary history, particularly following hybridization and introgression. By comparing these independent genomic histories, I am investigating whether discordant relationships reflect **incomplete lineage sorting, historical gene flow, or more recent hybridization**.

A particular focus is the complex of *P. phoxinus*, *P. csikii*, *P. morella*, and *P. septimaniae*, where geographic structure, mitochondrial ancestry, and nuclear genomic relationships do not always coincide.

The project also examines hybrid populations in the Rhine drainage, where contrasting mitochondrial and nuclear ancestry provides an opportunity to investigate the genomic consequences of **secondary contact between divergent lineages**.

### Computational approach

Rather than relying on a single statistic or phylogenetic method, the analysis integrates complementary approaches across multiple levels of genomic variation.

Genome-wide ancestry and population structure are combined with mitochondrial phylogenetics, nuclear phylogenomics, phylogenetic topology weighting, and model-based tests of incomplete lineage sorting and introgression.

This allows discordant genomic signals to be investigated from **individual ancestry and population structure through to genome-wide phylogenetic relationships**, providing multiple lines of evidence for distinguishing competing evolutionary scenarios.

### Computational toolkit

**Population genomics:** SNP analysis · population structure · ancestry estimation · hybrid index  
**Phylogenomics:** Mitochondrial phylogenetics · nuclear phylogenomics · species delimitation  
**Gene flow:** D-statistics · introgression analysis · QuIBL  
**Phylogenetic discordance:** TWISST · topology weighting · incomplete lineage sorting  
**Programming & workflow:** R · Python · Bash  
**HPC:** Linux · high-performance computing
